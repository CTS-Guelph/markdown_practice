# The Shallow Representation of Female Characters in Video Games
 
### *Written by Audrey Cullen*

For my final project within CTS1000: Keywords, I wanted to devote my time to a topic that I was not only interested in learning more about as well as its surrounding issues in a more in-depth analysis, but something that I felt moved by enough to talk about. I have decided to delve into the question as to how the lack of representation and the shallow, carnal design of female characters within video games contribute to gender-based discrimination and further foster environments of harm to women, both in the physical and digital world.

## Brief introduction and appropriate analysis of the keyword *gender*.
	
To begin, I wanted to examine an incredibly important keyword in relation to the number of issues linked to female characters within video games, gender. According to the general definitions I have chosen from the Oxford English Dictionary (gender, n. and gender, v^2), gender can be a distinctive term utilized to purely draw attention to the biological differences of sex such as physical aspects that make up our appearance (Oxford English Dictionary, n.d.). On the other hand, gender can also be a set of social, cultural roles, and behaviours that are quite often associated with or expected of individuals based on their status as male, female or non-binary. Its complexity ranges from its multiple ways of being spelled, to the diverse way it is used in linguistics as well as its undeniable societal burden as an oppressing label (Oxford English Dictionary, n.d.). Which in connection to my topic leads into the conversation that analyzes the way in which gender (being a woman) comes with its restrictions and limitations that dictate and decide what women get to/should be in a storytelling environment.
