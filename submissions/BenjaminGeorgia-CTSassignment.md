### Introduction

"The Anti Eviction Mapping Project" is a digital project that can use the framework provided by Temi Odumosu in the article "The Crying Child: On Colonial Archives, Digitization, and Ethics of Care in the Cultural Commons", drawing from the ideas that focus on the implementation of the ethics of care which aim to contextualize in a way that fosters compassion and is essential to marginalized groups, particularly in digital archiving, storytelling, and data collection and visualization practices. An additional framework of intersectionality explained in the article "Mapping the Margins: Intersectionality, Identity Politics, and Violence Against Women of Colour" written by Kimberlé Williams Crenshaw plays a key role as well in the understanding of the impact of this digital project on its ability to illuminate the overlap between multiple categories of identity, specifically in the context of residency systems working around or against these communities.
